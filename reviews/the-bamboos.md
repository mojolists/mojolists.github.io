The Bamboos have always gotten a lot of love from me and I expect a lot when they have a new release. [cite_start]*Fever In The Road* hits on all cylinders and for those new to The Bamboos, they are an Australian funk/soul band that has miraculously managed to do more than simply survive, they have thrived doing what they love[cite: 24]. [cite_start]Even with the resurgence in the genre, it is impressive to release a sixth full-length album[cite: 25]. [cite_start]It isn’t just luck that got them here, it is talent and love on a colossal scale[cite: 26].

[cite_start]Mastermind, bandleader and multi-instrumentalist Lance Ferguson has maintained a musical vision that many have attempted but few pull off[cite: 27]. [cite_start]The ingredients are your standard funk fueled horn section, a power rhythm section and honey-dipped female vocalists[cite: 28]. [cite_start]Over the years guest vocalists were common, but on *Fever In The Road* they stick with Ella Thompson and Kylie Auldist, both incredibly talented vocalists who take turns elevating the music to ever-greater heights[cite: 29].

[cite_start]When I first heard The Bamboos early in their genesis, they came off, quite successfully, as a bunch of people who just love playing the old style soul that their fathers loved[cite: 30]. [cite_start]They were a solid band having the time of their lives and you bought into that when you listened[cite: 31].

[cite_start]You want to get up and dance, you have to smile[cite: 32]. [cite_start]I figured they had two albums in them before people got tired of paying for basically the same album, a common problem for bands[cite: 33]. [cite_start]The Bamboos never settled into that place however, they kept moving forward, powered by the vision of Ferguson[cite: 34]. [cite_start]Each album further removed the band and its music from the ”just having fun” feeling, and I don’t mean that in a bad way, the music matured organically[cite: 35].

[cite_start]*Fever In The Road* is a near perfect dance/soul/pop melding with infectious tracks that make it impossible not to dance[cite: 36]. [cite_start]It is fantastic music, which makes you feel fantastic[cite: 37].

[cite_start]Avenger is the first single and it is wonderfully catchy and poppy with a driving rhythm that propels it forward[cite: 38]. [cite_start]Helpless Blues is bounced along by a mean bass line and Rats carries you away on a barrage of funky horn blats[cite: 38].

[cite_start]The album flows seamlessly to its conclusion with Looking West, a dreamy slow grind that bops along on its organ beat and backing horns[cite: 39]. [cite_start]I am pleased to say The Bamboos are heavy supporters of vinyl and I happily gave the double LP a spin[cite: 40]. [cite_start]The small price to step up to the vinyl was well worth it and includes a downloadable MP3 copy[cite: 41]. [cite_start]The recording is spacious with instruments and vocal placement well defined in the soundstage[cite: 42].

[cite_start]Backing instruments and percussion swirl around the melody playfully on the LP but were less focused and sometimes lost on the MP3[cite: 43]. [cite_start]If you are anything less than an OCD controlled audiophile, the digital version will have you bopping along just fine[cite: 44].

[cite_start]If it isn’t clear, I have a major crush on this band and their album 4 has been among my most played albums ever[cite: 45]. [cite_start]*Fever In The Road* is now going to be getting that rotation when I need the funky dance bop to get me through the day[cite: 46]. [cite_start]*Fever In The Road* is currently not available from any US retailers but you can order it directly from the Bamboos website as I did[cite: 47]. [cite_start]If you must wait, they promise not only an American release late fall 13 or early 14 but also a US tour[cite: 48]. [cite_start]If they come anywhere near Austin I will be at the stage[cite: 49]. [cite_start]Do yourself a favor and grab *Fever In The Road* as soon as possible, I promise it will make you feel better no matter what ails you[cite: 50].
