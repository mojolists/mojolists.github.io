## At Release

This collaborative project was a major head-scratcher for a lot of people in 1981. Fresh off their work together on Talking Heads' Remain in Light, the duo took analog tape loops of radio preachers, politicians, and global singers, layering them over incredibly dense, funky backing tracks. The press met it with a mixture of deep fascination and outright confusion.

While critics respected the sheer ambition of the recording, the idea of an album with no traditional "lead singer" was a tough concept for the average listener to grasp. The complex legal hurdles of clearing all those vocal recordings also added an extra layer of difficulty, making it seem more like an academic art project than a commercial pop release.

## The Influence

Released three years before hip-hop made sampling a mainstream conversation, this album invented the technique at a conceptual level — building songs entirely from found voices, radio broadcasts, field recordings, processed through funk and African rhythmic frameworks. It's the direct ancestor of every producer who treats the human voice as raw material. Paul Simon heard it and made Graceland. Peter Gabriel heard it and made So. The entire world music crossover movement of the 80s flows from this record.

They also proved that organic, live musicianship could be seamlessly integrated with electronic processing and looping. By running live drum kits and slapping basslines alongside rigid, repeated vocal phrases, they dissolved the boundary between a standard rock band and an electronic producer's studio.

## Where It Stands Today

Its legacy in the modern audio landscape is absolutely massive. Listening to it now, it doesn't sound jarring at all, primarily because the sampling techniques they pioneered have been adopted by almost every single genre of contemporary music.

It has smoothly transitioned from being viewed as an experimental novelty into a visionary document. It essentially predicted the future of digital production before the technology even fully existed, securing its place as a groundbreaking milestone in recording history.
