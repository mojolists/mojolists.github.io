Herbie Hancock's third album for Blue Note, recorded in a single session at **Rudy Van Gelder's Englewood Cliffs studio on August 30, 1963**. Hancock was 23 years old and had just joined Miles Davis's second great quintet that May — this record catches him between *My Point of View* and *Empyrean Isles*, deliberately stripping down to a quartet and pushing into Latin-percussion-forward territory.

The lineup is Hancock on piano, **Paul Chambers** on bass, **Willie Bobo** on drums, and **Osvaldo "Chihuahua" Martinez** on congas and bongo. Produced by Alfred Lion, engineered by Van Gelder — the definitive Blue Note production team at peak form.

**Cool stuff:**
- **Paul Chambers was recommended to Hancock by Miles Davis himself**. Chambers had been Miles's bassist since 1955, and this was the first time he'd recorded with Hancock
- Hancock's stated goal for the session was to release himself from customary jazz **"assumptions"** — no assumed chord changes, no assumed 4/4, no assumed walking bass. He gave the musicians a different small set of rules for each piece and told them to assume nothing else
- Of the five tracks, **only "Mimosa" was fully arranged**, with a symphonic intro. The other four — "Succotash," "Triangle," "Jack Rabbit," and "A Jump Ahead" — had no chord changes and often no written melodies going in
- **Willie Bobo** (born William Correa) came from years of work with Tito Puente and Cal Tjader — putting a Latin-percussion veteran on a Blue Note date was an unusual move for the label at the time
- Was reissued in the 1970s under the title ***Succotash***, with the sides reversed and credited jointly to Hancock and Bobo

**Spin it for:** Latin-percussion jazz, modal drift, the unmistakable Van Gelder room sound, and the sound of Hancock at 23 just before he changed jazz forever.

**Standouts:** "Succotash" · "Triangle" · "Jack Rabbit" · "Mimosa"
