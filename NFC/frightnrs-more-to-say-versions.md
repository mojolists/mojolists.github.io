A full-length **dub version** of The Frightnrs' 2016 debut *Nothing More To Say*, produced by **Victor "Ticklah" Axelrod** and released on Daptone in October 2017. The Frightnrs were a Queens-based quartet doing uncannily faithful late-'60s-style Jamaican rocksteady — Hammond organ, spring reverb, vocals that sounded airlifted from 1968. *More To Say Versions* strips out the lead vocals, blows out the low end, and runs the whole thing through Axelrod's dub board.

Everything here carries heavy context. The band's frontman, **Dan Klein, died of ALS on June 6, 2016 at age 33** — three months before the original *Nothing More To Say* was released. That record became a posthumous release; *More To Say Versions* is the second echo of it, arriving a year later.

**Cool stuff:**
- Producer **Victor "Ticklah" Axelrod** is a longtime Daptone associate (Antibalas, Sharon Jones & The Dap-Kings) with a parallel career as one of the most respected contemporary reggae/dub producers working. He dubbed the B-side of The Frightnrs' "Dispute" single; when that version hit, Daptone had him dub the whole album
- **Dan Klein was diagnosed with ALS in November 2015** — symptoms had begun that summer, just days after the band started recording *Nothing More To Say* with Axelrod. He kept singing through the sessions and the band completed the record before his death
- One of the **only reggae albums on the Daptone roster** — a label otherwise defined by deep-funk and soul — which made it a cult item inside the reggae community
- Recorded and mixed largely to **analog tape** at Daptone's House of Soul studio in Bushwick

**Spin it for:** deep-dub heaviness, rocksteady bass and organ, and a record that functions as both a killer late-night spin and a quiet act of mourning.
