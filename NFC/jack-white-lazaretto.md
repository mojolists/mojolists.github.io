Jack White's second solo album, released in June 2014 on his own Third Man Records. Produced at Third Man's Nashville HQ, it's a looser and more eclectic record than *Blunderbuss* — blues-rock, country shuffle, fuzz-guitar freakouts, and spoken-word interludes all elbowing for space. But the music is only half the story of this one. The **Ultra LP pressing** is the most over-engineered piece of vinyl ever commercially released.

**Cool stuff (the vinyl edition):**
- **Three-speed LP** — hidden tracks pressed under the center label play at 78 RPM on Side A and 45 RPM on Side B; the rest of the record plays at 33⅓
- **Side A plays from the inside out**, ending at a **locked groove on the outer edge**
- A **hologram of a spinning angel** is etched into the dead wax of Side A, visible when light hits it just right — designed by Tristan Duke at Infinity Light Science
- **"Just One Drink"** uses **dual-groove technology**: two intros (one electric, one acoustic) are cut into parallel grooves, so where you drop the needle determines which version you hear before they converge
- Sold **40,000 vinyl copies in its first week** — the most vinyl sales in a single week since SoundScan began tracking in 1991. The record stood for seven years until Taylor Swift's *Evermore* broke it in 2021

**Cool stuff (the music):**
- Debuted at **#1 on the Billboard 200**
- Several of the lyrics are drawn from **short stories and plays Jack White wrote when he was 19**, after he dropped out of Wayne State University. He found a box of the old writings in his attic and set pieces of them to music — describing it as "collaborating with your 19-year-old self"

**Spin it for:** raucous fuzz-blues, the single coolest-engineered vinyl release of the 21st century, and an excuse to show off the hologram to anyone in the room.

**Standouts:** "Lazaretto" · "Just One Drink" · "Temporary Ground" · "High Ball Stepper"
